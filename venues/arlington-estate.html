Featured Venue · Vaughan

Live Music at The Arlington Estate

The Arlington Estate is one of our favourite places to perform in the GTA — a seven-acre property in Vaughan that pairs a heritage 1856 house with clean, modern architecture. If you're getting married there, here's how a live harp & cello duo fits into the day.

8934 Huntington Rd, Vaughan, ON

A venue we genuinely love performing at

We don't say this about every venue, but The Arlington Estate is a real favourite — the grounds, the staff, and the way the property is laid out all make it an easy, beautiful place to play. The estate sits on seven acres with a private pond, manicured walking paths, and an on-site heritage house alongside modern East Wing and West Wing event spaces. Floor-to-ceiling windows carry natural light through the day, which matters more than couples expect: it's the same light your ceremony music and photos share.

Where live music fits at the Arlington

The estate's layout naturally separates the day into distinct spaces, which makes it easy to plan music around.

Ceremony

Outdoor garden or East/West Wing

The outdoor ceremony garden with its wooden pergola suits an acoustic harp and cello setup beautifully; both wings also work well for an indoor ceremony if that's your plan, or your weather backup.

Cocktail Hour

The terrace over the pond

The private terrace overlooking the pond is a favourite spot for us to set up — guests mingling with that view, and live music filling in the quiet.

Reception

Dinner in the East or West Wing

Both wings are column-free, so there's room to place us wherever suits your floor plan — background music through dinner, or a live arrangement for your entrance or first dance.

Why harp & cello suit this property

The Arlington Estate's mix of heritage architecture and modern, minimal interiors gives us a lot to work with. The harp carries the light, garden-ceremony feel the outdoor spaces call for, while the cello has enough warmth and presence to hold its own in the reception hall during dinner. Between the two instruments, we can move from a quiet processional to a fuller cocktail-hour set without ever needing amplification to be heard.
