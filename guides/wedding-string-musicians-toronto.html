Live Stringed Instruments for Your Toronto Wedding

We're The StringSisters, Naomi & Heather — a harp & cello duo performing at weddings across Toronto and the GTA. This is a full rundown of how live strings fit a wedding day: what a duo, trio, and quartet each sound like, where we typically play, what it costs to book, and the questions couples ask most before they do.

Based in Toronto · Performing GTA-wide

A harp & cello duo, and why we chose it

"Stringed instruments" covers a lot of ground — solo violin, a duo, a trio, a full string quartet. We perform specifically as a harp and cello duo, on purpose. Two instruments give you a genuinely full, layered sound: the harp carries the light, airy top end that suits a processional, while the cello adds the low warmth that fills a room during cocktail hour or dinner. It's a smaller footprint than a trio or quartet, which means we fit ceremony spaces a four-piece ensemble can't — a garden nook, a tight indoor aisle, a boat, a backyard — without losing the richness a solo instrument alone can't provide.

Duo, trio, or quartet? What actually differs

If you're comparing ensemble sizes while you plan, here's the honest breakdown of what changes as you add players.

Solo

One instrument

A single violin, cello, or harp gives a beautiful, intimate sound for a small ceremony or a quiet prelude, but it can thin out fast in a large room or over a long cocktail hour.

Duo — what we play

Harp & cello

Two complementary instruments give you a full, textured sound without a large footprint. It's the sweet spot for most Toronto ceremonies, cocktail hours, and intimate receptions — which is why it's what we specialize in.

Trio & Quartet

Three or four players

A string trio or full quartet (two violins, viola, cello) delivers a bigger, more orchestral sound — well suited to a large, formal ceremony hall — but needs more space, more chairs, and a bigger budget than a duo.

Where live strings fit into your wedding day

Most couples who book us use live music for one, two, or all three of these moments — you can mix and match based on your day and your budget.

Ceremony

Prelude, processional & recessional

Seated guests hear a prelude set as they arrive, then a processional as the wedding party enters, and a recessional as you walk back out as newlyweds — classical, contemporary, or a mix.

Cocktail Hour

Background music with a pulse

A relaxed but attentive set while guests mingle — usually a bit more upbeat and pop-leaning than the ceremony, without ever needing to compete for attention.

Reception

Dinner music & special moments

Soft background music through dinner that can rise into a live arrangement for your entrance, first dance, or cake cutting, before handing off to your DJ or band for dancing.

Repertoire: classical, pop, and everything you ask for

A harp and cello duo doesn't mean you're locked into Pachelbel and Bach — though those classics work beautifully for a processional. We regularly arrange contemporary pop, R&B, musical theatre, and film scores for harp and cello, and song requests are part of our normal planning process, not an upcharge. If you can name it, we can usually find or build an arrangement for it before your day.

Booking: timeline, packages, and what to expect

Popular GTA wedding dates — Saturdays from June through October especially — tend to book out 12 to 18 months ahead, so earlier is better if you have a date in mind, though we're glad to check for last-minute openings too. Packages are flexible: some couples book us for the ceremony only, others add cocktail hour, and some have us play through dinner. Pricing depends on how much of the day you'd like covered and how far your venue is from Toronto — the clearest way to get an exact number is to check our dates and request a quote.
